Two neighbouring states, two money lending Acts of almost the same vintage, and two completely
different exposures. In Tamil Nadu, lending without a licence is a fine of up to one thousand
rupees. **In Kerala it is imprisonment of not less than three months.**

That is not a detail. It is the whole reason "state money lending compliance" cannot be written
once and applied across the south, and it is why this page exists separately from
[the Tamil Nadu one](/blog/tamil-nadu-money-lending-licence/).

Everything below is quoted from the Kerala Money-Lenders Act, 1958 (Act 35 of 1958). **Read the
caution on currency at the end before relying on a figure.**

## Who needs a licence, and how many

**Section 3(1).** From the date the Act is brought into force in an area, *"no person, firm or joint
family [or unincorporated association of individuals] shall commence or carry on or continue
business as a money-lender at any place in such area without a licence obtained under this Act or
in contravention of the terms thereof."*

**With a proviso Tamil Nadu does not have:** *"nothing in this section shall be deemed to prohibit a
person who has applied for a licence to carry on or to continue business as a money-lender pending
orders on his application."* An applicant may trade while the application is pending.

**Section 3(2) — one licence per place of business**, in the same wording as Tamil Nadu: a lender
with more than one shop or place of business, in the same town or in different towns or villages,
*"shall obtain a separate licence in respect of each such shop or place of business."*

**Section 3(3)** sets who holds it: a registered firm in the firm's name; an undivided joint family
in the name of the manager, karanavan or yajaman, described as such; any other association of
individuals, a separate licence for each individual describing himself as a member.

## What the licence costs, and the condition attached to deposits

**Section 4(1):** the application is in writing to the licensing authority, in the prescribed manner.
A person under eighteen, or a lunatic as defined in the Indian Lunacy Act, 1912, may apply only
through a guardian — and a guardian who obtains a licence is *"subject to all the provisions of this
Act as if the licence has been granted to himself."*

**Section 4(2)** makes the licence subject to conditions including:

- *"payment of a licence fee of five thousand rupees"*;
- payment of security under sub-section (2A);
- and — the one worth stopping on — *"that deposits shall be accepted only in accordance with the
  provisions of the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934"*, at rates of interest not exceeding a
  specified level.

**A Kerala money-lender may take deposits, and the Act anticipates it.** Section 7(4) reinforces
this by prohibiting presents, gifts or commission *"to any depositor in connection with the deposits
received by such money-lender"*. This is a structural difference from Tamil Nadu, where the Act
contemplates lending only.

It is also where state and central regulation meet: the licence condition points at the RBI Act,
1934, so a Kerala money-lender accepting deposits is answering to both.

## The interest ceiling — and Kerala puts a formula in the Act

**Section 7(1):**

> *"No money-lender shall charge interest on any loan at a rate exceeding two per cent above the
> maximum rate of interest charged by commercial banks on loans granted by them: Provided that a
> money-lender shall be entitled to charge a minimum of one rupee as interest on any transaction."*

And a further proviso: *"the Government may specify, by notification, the rate of interest under
sub-section (1) from time to time."*

**So Kerala gives you both a formula and a notification power.** Tamil Nadu gives only the
notification power, with the rate "correlated to" RBI's bank rates and no formula at all. A lender
working across both states cannot carry one ceiling between them, and the Kerala figure moves with
commercial bank lending rates rather than with a gazette entry alone.

**Section 7(2):** charges as may be prescribed. **Section 7(3):** nothing in excess of
sub-section (1). **Section 7(4):** no presents, gifts or commission either way — to a depositor, or
from a borrower.

## The records a Kerala money-lender must keep

**Section 9(1)** — *"Every money-lender other than a pawn-broker shall"* — and that carve-out is
itself worth noting, because a Kerala pawn-broker is outside this section:

**(a) Keep an account for each debt separately**, showing:

- *"the date of the loan, the amount of the principal of the loan and the rate of interest charged
  on the loan"*; and
- *"the amount of every payment received by the money-lender in respect of the loan, and the date of
  such payment."*

Note the difference from Tamil Nadu: TN's clause (a)(i) also requires **the nature of the security
taken**. Kerala's does not. And Kerala says *"for each debt separately"* where Tamil Nadu says *"for
each debtor separately"* — a debtor with three loans is three accounts in Kerala.

**(b) Give a receipt for every payment**, *"duly signed and, if necessary, stamped at the time of
such payment."*

**(c) Furnish a statement of account on written requisition**, to the debtor or a person named by
him, signed, showing the clause (a) particulars and the amount outstanding on principal and
interest, for a prescribed fee.

**(d) Submit returns to the Inspector** — *"submit to the Inspector concerned such returns relating
to the loans advanced by him, in such form and at such times as may be prescribed."*

As in Tamil Nadu, every form, the return and its timing are in the **rules**, not the Act. A
compliance plan built from the Act alone is missing all of them.

## What happens when it goes wrong, and this is the part to read twice

**Section 17 — carrying on without a licence or in violation of its conditions:**

> *"shall be punished with imprisonment for a term which, in the absence of special reasons to be
> recorded in the judgement of the court, shall not be less than three months but which may extend
> to three years and with fine which may extend to fifty thousand rupees."*

**A mandatory minimum of three months' imprisonment**, displaceable only by special reasons recorded
in the judgment, and a fine of up to fifty thousand rupees. The Tamil Nadu equivalent is a fine of
up to one thousand rupees and no imprisonment.

Section 17 also catches lending *"in violation of the conditions of the licence"* — not only
unlicensed lending. A licensed lender breaching a condition is inside the same section.

**Section 18(1)** covers everything else: contravening the Act, the rules, or a licence condition,
or making a false claim or statement, where no other penalty is provided — fine up to one thousand
rupees. Its Explanation records that cancellation of a licence under section 14 *"shall not be
deemed to be a penalty"* for this purpose.

## Kerala and Tamil Nadu, side by side

| | Tamil Nadu (Act XXVI of 1957) | Kerala (Act 35 of 1958) |
|---|---|---|
| Interest ceiling | Notification only, *"correlated to"* RBI bank rates (s.7(1), as substituted 1979) | **Formula in the Act** — 2% above the maximum rate charged by commercial banks — plus a notification power (s.7(1)) |
| Minimum interest | — | **One rupee** per transaction |
| Licence fee | Conditions may include a fee *"not exceeding one hundred rupees"* | **Five thousand rupees** (s.4(2)(i)), plus security |
| Trading while the application is pending | — | **Permitted** (proviso to s.3(1)) |
| Deposits | Not contemplated | **Contemplated**, subject to the RBI Act, 1934 (s.4(2)) |
| Account kept per | **Debtor** | **Debt** |
| Nature of security recorded | **Yes** (s.9(1)(a)(i)) | No |
| Pawn-brokers | Within s.9 | **Excluded** from s.9 |
| Unlicensed lending | Fine up to **₹1,000** (s.17) | **Imprisonment 3 months to 3 years** and fine up to **₹50,000** (s.17) |

The last row is the one to act on. A lender treating "state money lending compliance" as one subject
across the two states is carrying a Tamil Nadu risk assessment into a jurisdiction with a mandatory
custodial minimum.

## And neither is RBI regulation

A money-lender licensed under a state Act is not an NBFC and files no DNBS returns. Nothing about
holding a state licence exempts a company from registration under the RBI Act if it crosses the
principal business test — the regimes answer different questions, and the Kerala deposit condition
is the one place they visibly touch. If what you are forming is an NBFC, that is a different path:
[NBFC registration and the Certificate of Registration](/blog/nbfc-registration-and-cor/).

## A caution on currency

The text read carries amendments through **1963, 1974, 1986 and 1987**, with several sections shown
as substituted. Before acting:

1. Check for **amendments after 1987**, including any made alongside Kerala's 2012 legislation on
   exorbitant interest, which defines its threshold by reference to section 7(1) of this Act.
2. Check **any notification** specifying the rate under the second proviso to section 7(1) — it
   overrides the formula.
3. Check the **rules** for the prescribed forms, the return format and its timing, and the
   prescribed fee under section 9(1)(c).
4. Section 4(2)(iii)'s deposit condition references a specified maximum rate of interest which the
   text I read truncates. Confirm it before accepting a deposit.

Written **9 October 2026**. Where this page quotes, it quotes the Act. Where the Act says
"prescribed", this page says so rather than supplying a figure from somewhere else.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the maximum interest a money lender can charge in Kerala?

Section 7(1) sets it at two per cent above the maximum rate of interest charged by commercial banks
on loans granted by them, with a minimum of one rupee per transaction. A further proviso lets the
Government specify the rate by notification, which would override the formula — so check for a
current notification before relying on the formula alone.

### Is unlicensed money lending a criminal offence in Kerala?

Yes, and it carries a custodial minimum. Section 17 provides imprisonment of not less than three
months, absent special reasons recorded in the judgment, extending to three years, with a fine up to
fifty thousand rupees. It applies to lending in violation of a licence condition as well as to
lending without one.

### Can a Kerala money lender accept deposits?

The Act contemplates it. Section 4(2) makes it a licence condition that deposits be accepted only in
accordance with the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934, and section 7(4) forbids presents, gifts or
commission to depositors. That is a point where the state licence and central regulation meet.

### Does a Kerala licence cover more than one branch?

No. Section 3(2) requires a separate licence for each shop or place of business, in the same town or
in different towns or villages.

### Can I lend while my licence application is pending?

The proviso to section 3(1) says the section does not prohibit a person who has applied for a licence
from carrying on or continuing business pending orders on the application. Tamil Nadu's Act has no
equivalent.
