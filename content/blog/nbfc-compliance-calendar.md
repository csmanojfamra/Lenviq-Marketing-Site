The Reserve Bank does not publish an NBFC compliance calendar. It publishes a **periodicity rule**,
and every date below is derived from it. That matters more than it sounds, because it means you can
check any line on this page without trusting the page — and it is why the calendars circulating with
pre-2024 dates on them are still circulating. Nobody re-derived them.

## The rule everything below comes from

Paragraph 4.4 of the [Master Direction — Reserve Bank of India (Filing of Supervisory Returns)
Directions, 2024](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12613)
(RBI/DoS.DSG/2023-24/110, **27 February 2024**) sets the deadline by how often a return is due, not
by which return it is:

- **Weekly** — reference date Friday, due *"on or before Wednesday of the following week"*
- **Fortnightly** — reference dates the 15th and the last day of the month, due *"within 7 days"*
- **Monthly** — reference date month end, due *"within 15 days"*
- **Quarterly** — reference date quarter end, due *"within 21 days"*
- **Half-yearly** — reference dates 31 March and 30 September, due *"within 21 days"*
- **Yearly** — reference date 31 March, due *"within 21 days"*

Audited returns are due *"within 5 working days from the date of signing of the Auditor's report"*,
and the returns in **Annex IV** have their own timelines which override the above.

That direction **repealed** the Master Direction — Non-Banking Financial Company Returns (Reserve
Bank) Directions, 2016. **If a calendar you are holding says a quarterly return is due in 15 days,
it predates February 2024.**

## Two things that make this calendar yours rather than generic

**Your layer decides which rows apply to you.** Base Layer, Middle Layer and Upper Layer file
different returns — see [NBFC category and layer](/blog/nbfc-category-and-layer/). A Base Layer NBFC
files DNBS02 and will never file DNBS01 or DNBS03.

**Two asset-size thresholds cut across the layers:** ₹100 crore for DNBS04A and DNBS04B, and
₹500 crore for the CRILC returns, the fraud returns and the Statutory Auditor Certificate. Below
those, the rows simply do not apply.

## The year, month by month

Dates are the statutory last day. Where one falls on a holiday, file earlier rather than assuming.

### April — the heaviest month

| Date | Falls due | Reference date |
|---|---|---|
| **15 April** | DNBS04B (monthly), DNBS08 (CRILC-Main) | 31 March |
| **21 April** | **Q4 quarterly returns** — DNBS01, DNBS02, DNBS03, DNBS04A, DNBS11, DNBS12, DNBS13, DNBS14, FMR-IV as applicable | 31 March |
| **21 April** | **Half-yearly** returns | 31 March |
| **21 April** | **Yearly** returns on the ordinary timeline | 31 March |
| Each Wednesday | DNBS09 (CRILC weekly) | preceding Friday |

April carries the quarterly, the half-yearly and the annual cycle on the same date, because all
three share a 31 March reference date and the same 21-day window. It is the month to staff.

### May, June

| Date | Falls due | Reference date |
|---|---|---|
| **15 May** | DNBS04B, DNBS08 | 30 April |
| **15 June** | DNBS04B, DNBS08 | 31 May |
| Each Wednesday | DNBS09 | preceding Friday |

### July

| Date | Falls due | Reference date |
|---|---|---|
| **15 July** | DNBS04B, DNBS08 | 30 June |
| **21 July** | **Q1 quarterly returns** | 30 June |
| Each Wednesday | DNBS09 | preceding Friday |

### August, September

| Date | Falls due | Reference date |
|---|---|---|
| **15 August** | DNBS04B, DNBS08 | 31 July |
| **15 September** | DNBS04B, DNBS08 | 31 August |
| Each Wednesday | DNBS09 | preceding Friday |

September is crowded for an NBFC for reasons that are **not** RBI's — the AGM, AOC-4 and MGT-7
under the Companies Act, 2013, DIR-3 KYC, and the tax audit. Those are real and they are not on this
calendar, deliberately: see *What is not here*.

### October

| Date | Falls due | Reference date |
|---|---|---|
| **15 October** | DNBS04B, DNBS08 | 30 September |
| **21 October** | **Q2 quarterly returns** | 30 September |
| **21 October** | **Half-yearly** returns | 30 September |
| Each Wednesday | DNBS09 | preceding Friday |

### November, December

| Date | Falls due | Reference date |
|---|---|---|
| **15 November** | DNBS04B, DNBS08 | 31 October |
| **15 December** | DNBS04B, DNBS08 | 30 November |
| **31 December** | **DNBS10 — Statutory Auditor Certificate**, outer limit | 31 March |
| Each Wednesday | DNBS09 | preceding Friday |

**DNBS10 is the one date on this calendar that is not derived from the periodicity rule.** It is an
Annex IV return, filed by the statutory auditor, due **within 5 working days of the date of signing
of the auditor's report** under section 134 of the Companies Act, 2013 — **and in any case not later
than 31 December**. So its real due date is set by when your audit is signed, and 31 December is only
the backstop. An NBFC whose accounts are signed in September has a September deadline, not a
December one.

### January

| Date | Falls due | Reference date |
|---|---|---|
| **15 January** | DNBS04B, DNBS08 | 31 December |
| **21 January** | **Q3 quarterly returns** | 31 December |
| Each Wednesday | DNBS09 | preceding Friday |

### February, March

| Date | Falls due | Reference date |
|---|---|---|
| **15 February** | DNBS04B, DNBS08 | 31 January |
| **15 March** | DNBS04B, DNBS08 | 28/29 February |
| Each Wednesday | DNBS09 | preceding Friday |

March has no quarterly filing in it. The 31 March reference date falls due in April.

## The ones with no date until something happens

| Obligation | Timeline |
|---|---|
| **FMR-I** — actual or suspected fraud | **Within three weeks of the date of detection** |
| **FMR-III** — update to FMR-I | Immediately, on a progress update |
| **Form A Certificate** — auditor appointment | **Within one month of the date of appointment** |
| **DNBS13** — overseas investment | Quarterly, **and a NIL return is required** where there are none |

DNBS13 belongs in this list as much as in the quarterly one, because an NBFC with no overseas
investment often does not realise it has a quarterly filing at all.

## What is not here, and why that is deliberate

**Companies Act, income tax and GST filings.** AOC-4, MGT-7, DIR-3 KYC, the AGM, TDS returns,
GSTR-3B. Every one is a real obligation and not one of them is RBI's. Calendars that merge the two
leave the impression that the Reserve Bank sets the AGM date, and a compliance officer who inherits
that impression will eventually explain it to an inspecting officer.

**Deposit-taking obligations.** This is the non-deposit-taking position.

**Housing Finance Companies.** HFCs are **excluded** from the NBFC category in the 2024 Directions.
Nothing here applies to them.

**Credit information reporting to the CICs.** It belongs on a compliance calendar and it is not in
the Supervisory Returns Directions — it sits under the Credit Information Companies (Regulation) Act,
2005 and the directions under it, and the Reserve Bank tightened its cadence in 2024. I have **not**
verified the current cadence from primary text for this page, so rather than print a date I cannot
stand behind: check it directly. It is the line most often stale in circulating calendars.

## On sourcing, plainly

The periodicity rule, the scope and the repeal were read from the Reserve Bank's own Master
Directions page. **The return list and the applicability thresholds were read from a verbatim
reproduction of Annex III, not from the Reserve Bank's PDF.** They agree with the primary page
everywhere the two overlap. Before you rely on a threshold to decide whether a return applies to
you, open [Annex III](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12613) and read
the row.

And the obvious caution: this page was written on **9 October 2026**. Every direction cited carries
its own date, so you can tell what has had time to change.
