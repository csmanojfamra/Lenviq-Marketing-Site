A money lender in Tamil Nadu has real compliance. It is simply **state** compliance rather than the
Reserve Bank's — a licence, prescribed records, receipts, a statement of account on demand, and
returns to an Inspector. None of it comes from an RBI direction and all of it is enforceable.

This page is read from [the Tamil Nadu Money-Lenders Act, 1957](https://www.cra.tn.gov.in/tnscs/Files/act/006a_The%20Tamil%20Nadu%20Money%20Lenders%20Act.pdf)
(Tamil Nadu Act XXVI of 1957) itself, and quotes it. **Read the caution on currency at the end before you rely on a figure** — it matters
more here than it does for an RBI direction.

## Who needs a licence, and how many

**Section 3(1).** No person shall, on and after the date the Act is brought into force in an area,
*"carry on, or continue to carry on, business as a money-lender at any place in such area, except
under and in accordance with the terms of licence."*

**One licence per place of business — section 3(2).** *"Where a money-lender has more than one shop
or place of business, whether in the same town or village or in different towns or villages he
shall obtain a separate licence in respect of each such shop or place of business."*

This is the provision most often missed by a lender who opens a second counter. It is not a branch
endorsement on one licence; it is a separate licence.

**Section 3(3) sets who holds it**, by the form the business takes:

- a **registered firm** — the licence is in the firm's name;
- an **undivided Hindu family** — in the name of the manager, karnavan or yajaman, described as such
  in the licence;
- **any other association of individuals** not required to be registered under the Companies Act —
  *"a separate licence shall be obtained by each such individual in his name describing himself as a
  member of the association."*

**And a change of premises is not automatic.** Section 5: no money-lender shall change his place of
business *"without previous notice to the licensing authority and without having the address of the
new place of business duly endorsed on his licence."*

**Section 6** requires the name to be kept exhibited over the shop.

## The interest ceiling is not in the Act

This is the single most-misreported thing about money lending in Tamil Nadu, and it is worth being
precise.

**Section 7(1)**, as substituted by section 4 of the Tamil Nadu Money-lenders (Amendment) Act, 1979
(Tamil Nadu Act 41 of 1979):

> *"No money lender shall charge interest on any loan at a rate exceeding such rate as the
> Government may, by notification, fix from time to time. Provided that the rate of interest as may
> be fixed by the Government shall be correlated to the current bank rates of lending as may be
> fixed by the Reserve Bank of India, from time to time."*

So the ceiling is **a government notification, correlated to RBI's bank rates** — not a number in
the statute. Any guide that quotes you a percentage "under the Tamil Nadu Money-Lenders Act" is
either quoting a notification without saying so, or quoting the pre-1979 position.

**What to do with that, practically:** the rate you may charge is whatever the current notification
fixes, and it moves. A lender who set a rate once against a figure found online is exposed to every
revision since.

**Charges are separate and also prescribed.** Section 7(2): *"A money-lender may demand and take
from the debtor such charges and in such cases, as may be prescribed."* And section 7(3) closes it:
*"A money-lender shall not demand or take from the debtor any interest, profit or other sum
whatsoever in excess of that payable under sub-section (1)."*

## The records, which are the real daily obligation

**Section 9(1)** is the heart of the Act for anybody actually running a book. Every money-lender
shall:

**(a) Keep an account per debtor**, showing *for each debtor separately*:

- *"the date of the loan, the amount of the principal of the loan, the rate of interest charged on
  the loan and the nature of security taken, if any"*; and
- *"the amount of every payment received by the money-lender in respect of the loan, and the date of
  such payment."*

Note what that is: a **per-debtor ledger**, not a cash book. Four fields at origination and two per
receipt.

**(b) Give a receipt for every payment** — *"a receipt for every amount paid by him, duly signed and
if necessary, stamped at the time of such payment."* At the time of payment, signed. Not a monthly
statement in place of receipts.

**(c) Furnish a statement of account on written requisition.** On the debtor's written requisition,
to the debtor or to a person named in it, signed, showing the particulars in clause (a) and the
amount outstanding on account of principal and interest — for a fee the Government may prescribe.

With one escape: *"no such statement shall be required to be furnished to a debtor if he is supplied
by the money-lender with a pass-book in the prescribed form containing an up-to-date account of the
money-lender's transactions with the debtor."*

**A pass-book in the prescribed form replaces the statement-on-demand obligation.** That is a
practical reason to issue pass-books rather than a courtesy.

**(d) Submit returns to the Inspector** — *"submit such returns relating to the loans advanced by him
to the Inspector concerned, in such form and at such times as may be prescribed."*

**And section 9(2):** *"All records or entries made in the books, accounts and documents referred to
in sub-section (1) shall be in such language as may be prescribed in respect of any area."*

That last one is easy to overlook and is a records-design decision, not a formatting preference.

**Where the forms live.** The Act says "prescribed" throughout — the forms, the pass-book, the
returns and their timing are in the **Tamil Nadu Money-Lenders Rules, 1959**, not in the Act. A
compliance plan built from the Act alone will be missing every form it needs.

## What happens when it goes wrong

**Section 17 — carrying on without a licence:** *"Whoever carries on the business of money-lending
without a licence or otherwise than in conformity with the terms and conditions of a licence shall
be punished with fine which may extend to one thousand rupees"*, with a proviso protecting somebody
who has ceased lending and is only recovering existing loans.

**A fine capped at one thousand rupees** is what the text I read says, and it is worth pausing on
rather than repeating cheerfully: a 1957 ceiling that has not kept pace is exactly the kind of
figure a later amendment revises. Treat it as the position in the text I could verify, not as
today's exposure. See the caution below.

**Section 14** empowers the licensing authority to **cancel or suspend** a licence, and sub-section
(2-A) allows suspension pending cancellation, for reasons recorded in writing, **with no show cause
notice**. An appeal lies to the prescribed authority within one month (section 14(3)).

**Section 15** requires every cancellation or suspension to be notified **in the District Gazette**
and on the notice board of the licensing authority's office. **Section 16**: no compensation beyond
a refund of the licence fee.

The reputational mechanism there is worth noticing. The penalty in section 17 is small; publication
in the District Gazette is not.

## Kerala is next door and the exposure is not the same

Worth knowing before you carry a Tamil Nadu assessment across the border. Under the Kerala
Money-Lenders Act, 1958, lending without a licence — or in violation of a licence condition —
carries **imprisonment of not less than three months**, extending to three years, with a fine up to
fifty thousand rupees (s.17). The Tamil Nadu equivalent, quoted above, is a fine of up to one
thousand rupees.

Kerala also writes its rate ceiling as a **formula in the Act** (two per cent above the maximum rate
charged by commercial banks), charges a **five thousand rupee** licence fee, permits trading while a
licence application is pending, contemplates **deposits** subject to the RBI Act, 1934, and requires
an account per **debt** rather than per **debtor**.

The full comparison is in [the Kerala page](/blog/kerala-money-lending-licence/). And
[West Bengal](/blog/west-bengal-money-lending-licence/) is a third shape again: the criminal penalty
there is a ₹200 fine, but an unlicensed lender **cannot obtain a decree at all** (s.13), which makes
the book unrecoverable rather than the lender fineable.

The point for anyone operating across them: these are three regimes, not one subject with local
variations.

## Why this is not the same question as RBI registration

A money lender licensed under a state Act is **not** an NBFC and is not supervised by the Reserve
Bank. There are no DNBS returns, no asset classification direction, no Scale Based Regulation layer.
Equally, nothing about holding a state licence exempts a company from the RBI Act's registration
requirement if it crosses the principal business test — the two regimes answer different questions
and a lender can be caught by both.

If what you are actually forming is an NBFC, that is a different path:
[NBFC registration and the Certificate of Registration](/blog/nbfc-registration-and-cor/).

## A caution on currency, which is not boilerplate here

The text above was read from a published copy of the Act carrying amendments up to the early
1980s — section 7(1) as substituted in 1979, and amendment references as late as 1982.

**Three things therefore need checking against the current official text before you act on them:**

1. **Any later amendment** to the sections quoted. State Acts are amended by state legislation that
   is less visible online than an RBI circular.
2. **The current notification fixing the maximum rate** under section 7(1). It is not in the Act and
   it changes.
3. **The [Tamil Nadu Money-Lenders Rules, 1959](https://www.cra.tn.gov.in/tnscs/Files/act/006b_Tamil%20Nadu%20Money-Lenders%20Rules%201959.pdf)**
   for the prescribed forms, the pass-book, the return format and its timing, and the prescribed
   language for an area.

Written **9 October 2026**. Where this page quotes, it quotes the Act. Where the Act says
"prescribed", this page says so rather than filling in a figure — because the figure is in a rule or
a notification, and quoting one without its date is how the guides this is meant to improve on went
wrong.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a money lender in Tamil Nadu need one licence or one per branch?

One per place of business. Section 3(2) is explicit: a money-lender with more than one shop or place
of business, in the same town or in different towns, must obtain a separate licence for each.

### What is the maximum interest rate under the Tamil Nadu Money-Lenders Act?

The Act does not state one. Section 7(1), as substituted in 1979, says the Government fixes the
maximum rate by notification from time to time, correlated to the Reserve Bank's bank rates. A
percentage quoted as being "under the Act" is either a notification quoted without its date or the
pre-1979 position.

### What records must a Tamil Nadu money lender keep?

Under section 9(1): an account for each debtor separately, showing the date and principal of the
loan, the rate of interest and the nature of any security, plus every payment and its date; a signed
receipt for every payment at the time it is made; a statement of account on the debtor's written
requisition, unless a pass-book in the prescribed form is supplied; and returns to the Inspector in
the prescribed form and at prescribed times. The entries must be in the prescribed language for the
area.

### Is a money lender regulated by the Reserve Bank of India?

No. A state money lending licence is state regulation. There are no RBI returns and no asset
classification direction attached to it. That is separate from the question of whether a company is
carrying on business as a non-banking financial institution, which has its own test.

### Does a pass-book remove any obligation?

Yes, one. Under the proviso to section 9(1)(c), a debtor supplied with a pass-book in the prescribed
form containing an up-to-date account need not be furnished with a statement of account on
requisition.
