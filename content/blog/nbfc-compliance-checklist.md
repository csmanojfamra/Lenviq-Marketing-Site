Most compliance checklists are arranged by subject — returns here, KYC there, governance somewhere
below. That is how the law is written and it is not how the work is done. The question a compliance
officer actually has on a Monday morning is *what falls due this week*, and a list arranged by
subject cannot answer it without being read end to end.

So this one is arranged by **frequency**. Each item names the direction it comes from and that
direction's date, because a compliance list without sources is an assertion, and an NBFC cannot file
an assertion.

> **One thing to settle before the list.** If you are working from a checklist written before
> **27 February 2024**, its filing deadlines are wrong. On that date the Reserve Bank issued the
> [Master Direction — Reserve Bank of India (Filing of Supervisory Returns) Directions,
> 2024](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12613)
> (RBI/DoS.DSG/2023-24/110 and DoS.DSG.No.10/33.01.001/2023-24), which **repealed** the Master
> Direction — Non-Banking Financial Company Returns (Reserve Bank) Directions, 2016 and harmonised
> every timeline in it. Several widely-circulated calendars still carry the pre-2024 dates.

## What the frequency rule actually is

This is the part worth memorising, because once you have it you can date any return without looking
anything up. Paragraph 4.4 of the 2024 Directions sets the timeline by **periodicity**, not by
return:

| Periodicity | Reference date | Due |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly | Friday of the week | *"On or before Wednesday of the following week"* |
| Fortnightly | 15th and last day of the month | *"Within 7 days from the Reference Date"* |
| Monthly | Last day of the month | *"Within 15 days from the Reference Date"* |
| Quarterly | Last day of the calendar quarter | *"Within 21 days from the Reference Date"* |
| Half-yearly | 31 March and 30 September | *"Within 21 days from the Reference Date"* |
| Yearly | 31 March | *"Within 21 days from the Reference Date"* |

Two riders. Audited returns are due *"within 5 working days from the date of signing of the
Auditor's report"*. And paragraph 4.4.2 provides **alternate timelines** for the returns listed in
Annex IV, which override the table above — the Statutory Auditor Certificate is one of them, and it
is dealt with under *Annual* below.

The quarterly figure is the one most often got wrong. It is **21 days**, not 15. It was 15 before
February 2024 for most of these returns, and that is why the older calendars disagree.

## Weekly

**DNBS09 — CRILC Weekly (RDB return).** Large borrowers who defaulted, or came out of default,
during the week. Reference date is the Friday; due on or before the Wednesday following.

Who files it: NBFC-Upper Layer; NBFC-Middle Layer except CICs; and NBFC-Base Layer entities that are
ICCs, MFIs or Factors **with asset size of ₹500 crore and above**. A Base Layer NBFC below that
threshold does not file it at all.

## Monthly

**DNBS04B — Structural Liquidity and Interest Rate Sensitivity.** Due within 15 days of month end.

**DNBS08 — CRILC-Main.** Credit information on borrowers with aggregate exposure of **₹5 crore and
above**. Due within 15 days of month end. Same applicability as DNBS09.

**Credit information reporting to the credit information companies.** This one is not in the
Supervisory Returns Directions — it sits under the Credit Information Companies (Regulation) Act,
2005 and the directions made under it, and its cadence was tightened to a fortnightly minimum by the
Reserve Bank in 2024. **Check the current cadence against the CIC directions before relying on a
monthly assumption**; it is the single most commonly out-of-date line in circulating checklists, and
I have not verified the current text for this page.

## Quarterly

Reference date is the quarter end — 30 June, 30 September, 31 December, 31 March — and the return is
due **within 21 days**. So: 21 July, 21 October, 21 January, 21 April.

Which return you file depends on your layer, and that is the whole of the design:

| Return | Covers | Who files |
|---|---|---|
| **DNBS01** | Assets and liabilities, profit and loss, sensitive-sector and sectoral exposure | NBFC-UL and NBFC-ML, except CICs |
| **DNBS02** | Financial details and compliance with prudential norms | **NBFCs in the Base Layer**, except P2Ps |
| **DNBS03** | Prudential parameters — capital adequacy, provisioning | NBFC-UL and NBFC-ML, except CICs |
| **DNBS04A** | Short Term Dynamic Liquidity | NBFC-UL; NBFC-ML except standalone primary dealers; **NBFC-BL with asset size ₹100 crore and above** |
| **DNBS11 / DNBS12** | CIC financial and prudential parameters | NBFC-CICs |
| **DNBS13** | Overseas investment details | **All NBFCs** — and a **NIL return is required** where there are none |
| **DNBS14** | P2P financial and prudential parameters | NBFC-P2Ps |
| **FMR-IV** | Dacoities, robberies, theft, burglaries | Same applicability as FMR-I |

**DNBS02 is the one to get right if you are a Base Layer NBFC**, and it is also the one the stale
calendars misstate most often. Under the 2024 Directions it is **quarterly, within 21 days**. Before
February 2024 it was an annual return due within 60 days. Two widely-cited filing guides still
describe it the old way, and they contradict each other.

**DNBS13 catches people out** because a NIL return is still a return. An NBFC with no overseas
investment has a quarterly filing obligation it may not know about.

## Half-yearly

Reference dates 31 March and 30 September, due within 21 days.

## Annual

**DNBS10 — Statutory Auditor Certificate (SAC).** Reference date 31 March. This is an Annex IV
return, so the alternate timeline applies: due **within 5 working days of the date of signing of the
auditor's report** under section 134 of the Companies Act, 2013 — **and in any case not later than
31 December** of the same year. Filed by the statutory auditor. Applies to all NBFCs and ARCs of
₹500 crore and above.

**Form A Certificate.** Certificate on appointment of the Statutory Central Auditor or Statutory
Auditor. Reference date 31 March, and the Annex IV timeline is **within one month of the date of
appointment**. All NBFCs.

## As and when it happens

**FMR-I — report on actual or suspected frauds.** Annex IV timeline: **within three weeks of the
date of detection**. NBFC-UL, NBFC-ML, and Base Layer ICCs, MFIs and Factors of ₹500 crore and above.

**FMR-III — update of FMR-I.** Due immediately on a progress update.

## The standing obligations that are not returns

A return is a thing you file. These are things that must be true continuously, and an inspection
tests them by asking for the document and the board resolution behind it.

**Know Your Customer.** The governing text for an NBFC is now the **Non-Banking Financial Companies —
Know Your Customer Directions, 2025**, dated **28 November 2025** (RBI/DOR/2025-26/361). It applies
to all categories of NBFC across all layers unless otherwise specified, and does not apply to an
NBFC with no customer interface. It was amended on **29 December 2025** (RBI/2025-26/160), which
added an explanation to paragraph 63 on responsibility for records in the Central KYC Records
Registry: the regulated entity that **last uploaded or updated** a customer's KYC information in
CKYCR is responsible for verifying identity and address, and an NBFC that downloads and relies on
such a record need not re-verify, where the record is current and compliant with the PML Act and
Rules.

*If your KYC policy still cites the 2016 Master Direction, that is the thing to look at first.* I
have **not** verified from the 2025 Directions' own repeal clause whether the 2016 NBFC KYC direction
was expressly repealed or superseded — the Reserve Bank's repeal of the equivalent **commercial bank**
direction on the same date is clearly documented, but the NBFC position I could not confirm from
primary text. Confirm it before amending a policy document on the strength of this paragraph.

**Scale Based Regulation.** The framework an NBFC's obligations hang off is the **Master Direction —
Reserve Bank of India (Non-Banking Financial Company — Scale Based Regulation) Directions, 2023**
(RBI/DoR/2023-24/106, DoR.FIN.REC.No.45/03.10.119/2023-24), dated **19 October 2023** and updated as
on **17 July 2025**, issued in supersession of the 2016 NBFC-NSI and NBFC-SI/Deposit-taking
directions. Your **layer** decides which of the returns above you file, and the applicability column
in each table is unreadable without knowing it.

I have deliberately **not** reproduced the Net Owned Fund threshold, the CRAR minimum or the layer
placement criteria here. They are in the Directions' PDF, which I did not read for this page, and
every figure for them that circulates in secondary sources is one I could not verify. A wrong
capital figure in a compliance checklist is worse than an absent one.

**Asset classification and income recognition.** Classification is computed in the **day-end
process** for the relevant date, and an NPA is upgraded only when the **entire arrears** of interest
and principal are paid — [RBI/2021-2022/125,
DOR.STR.REC.68/21.04.048/2021-22](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=12194&Mode=0),
dated 12 November 2021. That is covered at length in
[why an NPA figure run at 11am differs from the same figure at day-end](/blog/irac-day-end-classification/).

**Penal charges.** A penal amount is a *charge*, not interest, and may not be capitalised — the
April 2024 position, covered in [penal charges are not
interest](/blog/penal-charges-not-interest/).

**The Key Facts Statement.** What must be disclosed, and how the APR in it is built —
[the KFS requirement](/blog/kfs-key-facts-statement-nbfc-requirement/) and
[what goes in the APR](/blog/kfs-what-goes-in-the-apr/).

## What this page does not cover, and why

- **Companies Act and tax filings.** An NBFC is a company and files AOC-4, MGT-7, DIR-3 KYC, TDS
  returns and GST like any other. They are real obligations and they are not RBI's; a checklist that
  mixes them in without saying so produces the impression that the Reserve Bank sets the AGM date.
- **Deposit-taking obligations.** The returns above are the non-deposit position. A deposit-taking
  NBFC has more, and the set differs enough that mixing them would mislead.
- **HFCs.** Housing Finance Companies are **excluded** from the 2024 Supervisory Returns Directions'
  NBFC category. Their returns are a separate regime and nothing on this page applies to them.
- **Anything I could not source.** Noted inline above rather than paraphrased into confidence.

## A note on sourcing

The scope, the repeal and the frequency table above were read from the Reserve Bank's own Master
Directions page for the 2024 Directions. **The Annex III return list — which return, who files it —
was read from a verbatim reproduction of the Directions rather than from the Reserve Bank's own PDF.**
It is consistent with the primary page on every point the two overlap, but before you rely on an
applicability threshold for a filing decision, open
[Annex III](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12613) and check the row.

Regulation moves. Every direction cited here carries its date so you can tell how old this page is
relative to what you are reading it against.


## Frequently asked questions

### Are quarterly NBFC returns due in 15 days or 21 days?

Twenty-one days from the reference date, under paragraph 4.4 of the RBI (Filing of Supervisory
Returns) Directions, 2024. It was fifteen for most of these returns before 27 February 2024, which
is why older checklists disagree — and why a calendar quoting fifteen days can be dated at a glance.

### Which returns does a Base Layer NBFC actually file?

DNBS02 quarterly, DNBS13 quarterly including a NIL return where there are no overseas investments,
and Form A annually on auditor appointment. DNBS04A and DNBS04B begin at an asset size of Rs 100
crore; the CRILC returns, the fraud returns and the Statutory Auditor Certificate begin at Rs 500
crore. Below those thresholds the rows do not apply.

### Does an NBFC with no overseas investment still file DNBS13?

Yes. A NIL return is required. It is the obligation most often missed, for the obvious reason that
there is nothing to report.

### Do these obligations apply to a Housing Finance Company?

No. HFCs are excluded from the NBFC category in the 2024 Directions, and their returns are a
separate regime.
