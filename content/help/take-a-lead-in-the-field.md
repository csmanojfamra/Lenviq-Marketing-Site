A lead taken on paper is a lead somebody keys in later, usually the next morning, usually from
handwriting. The field app takes it where the conversation happens.

## Open the field app

Sign in at the same address as the desktop; a field officer lands on the round rather than the
dashboard. The app works with no signal — anything recorded is held on the phone and sent when a
tower appears.

## Enter the number first

The duplicate check runs while the officer is still typing. A borrower the lender already knows is
found *now*, while the officer is standing in front of them, rather than after the lead is saved —
which is a merge, and somebody's afternoon.

## What is asked, and what is not

Mobile, name, the lead's source and what the loan is for. The amount and tenure are indicative:
the branch decides the scheme. Nothing that needs a document is asked at a gate.

## Create the customer there

A lead becomes a **customer** first and a loan file after that. The customer record is what
everything else hangs from — KYC, addresses, identity documents, income and bank details all belong
to it, not to the application.

The pincode fills the state, district and town from the pincode master, so matching the branch's own
address form costs one box rather than four.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a lead be taken with no network?

No, and deliberately. The duplicate check runs against the book as it is now, and a lead queued for
hours would be checked against a book that has moved on — which is the one thing the check exists to
prevent. Collections queue offline; leads do not.

### Does the branch have to re-enter anything?

No. The PAN typed at the door is not asked for again at the PAN card step, and the four things a KYC
form always wants — surname, father's name, date of birth, marital status — are carried if the
officer could ask them.
