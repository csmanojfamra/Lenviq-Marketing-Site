# Beating allcloud, finsta and intelligrow — what the research actually found, and the plan

**Researched:** 9 October 2026, from their own sitemaps, their own pages, and live SERPs.
**Companion:** `docs/COMPETITOR_MAP.md` (positioning and vocabulary). This file is about *winning*.

---

## 0. Three findings that change the brief's premise

### 0.1 None of the three holds the head term. Aggregators do.

Searched `NBFC software India loan management system for NBFC`. The results are **listicles and
directories** — `engineerbabu.com` "Best Loan Management Software in India for NBFCs & Lenders
(2026)", GoodFirms, Capterra, ProductHunt — plus **larger vendors**: M2P Fintech, Nelito (FinCraft),
Finflux, TurnKey Lender, Roopya. Intelligrow appears, through `?p=10996`-style URLs.

**allcloud did not appear at all**, despite 127 URLs and active publishing.

Same shape for microfinance: **M2P Fintech's** 2026 guide, intelligrow's microfinance page,
twinline.org, decentro.

**So "beat allcloud" is the wrong objective for the head term, because allcloud does not hold it.**
Category head terms in software are won by review sites, and a vendor page rarely displaces one. The
realistic positions are (a) be *listed in* those directories, and (b) win everything that is not the
head term.

### 0.2 A mistake of mine, corrected before it reached the plan

allcloud's sitemap gives every one of its 75 blog posts the same `lastmod` of **2024-09-12**, which
reads as a blog abandoned two years ago. I nearly built a "beat them on freshness" plan on it.

**It is false.** Their blog shows posts through **1 October 2026**, published roughly monthly,
including *"What is NBFC Software? A Working Guide for Indian Lenders"* (Oct 2026) and
*"NBFC Software Pricing in India: What Drives the Cost"* (June 2026). The uniform `lastmod` is a
sitemap artefact.

**allcloud is the strongest of the three and is actively publishing.** Any plan that assumes
otherwise is wrong.

### 0.3 Lenviq's problem is not missing pages

Before proposing more content I checked what exists. The commercial-intent slots are **already
filled**: `how-to-choose-loan-management-software-nbfc`, `best-loan-management-software-india`,
`nbfc-software-complete-guide`, `spreadsheets-vs-loan-management-software-nbfc`,
`loan-management-software-for-nbfc`. Five pages, each claiming its own primary keyword.

**So the gap is authority, not coverage.** Seventeen commercial and regulatory pages sit on a site
with about eleven non-brand clicks in five and a half weeks. Writing an eighteenth does not change
that arithmetic. This is the single most important sentence in this document.

## 1. What each competitor actually is

### allcloud.in — the one to take seriously

- **127 URLs.** 7 product pages, 7 loan-segment pages, 75 blog posts, 12 tag pages, case studies,
  webinars, a video series, two gated ebooks.
- **Publishing monthly through October 2026.**
- **Real proof:** SOC 2 Type 2, ISO 27001, VAPT, Great Place to Work, Gartner/Capterra/G2/
  SoftwareSuggest badges. Also "110+ Lending businesses", "45 Lakh Loans empowered".
- **Deliberately chasing commercial intent**, including a pricing guide and a pricing blog post —
  the `nbfc software price in india` autocomplete the Search Console work found.
- **Weakness, and it is one weakness:** its pages carry no regulation. Its microfinance page names
  no direction, no household income assessment, no collateral-free rule, and never says JLG.

### intelligrow.co — large, and a different business

- **515 URLs**, and the composition matters: **212 are programmatic location pages** —
  `/location/kenya/nairobi/`, `/location/india/bengaluru/`, 17 regions, 95 countries, 100 cities.
  That is a doorway-page farm, the exact pattern the brief ruled out for Lenviq, and it inflates
  their URL count far beyond their real footprint.
- **232 blog posts**, heavily about **Mifos and Apache Fineract**. Eleven dedicated Mifos service
  pages — consulting, implementation, migration, upgrades.
- **They are a Mifos implementation services firm** wearing a product site. Their case studies are
  mostly Mifos engagements.
- **Weakness:** no certifications, no named customers on the homepage, and a product claim resting
  on open-source someone else maintains.

### finsta.in — small, and not the threat

- **29 pages total. Six blog posts, undated.**
- **Its strength is social proof**, not content: 16 named client logos and three named testimonials
  from credit co-operatives.
- It owns the **credit co-operative society** vocabulary, which Lenviq is not pursuing.
- **Not a content competitor.** Ranking past it is a question of having more than 29 pages, which
  Lenviq already does.

> *A note on the name:* "finstra" in the request is read as **finsta.in**, the Indian vendor on the
> brief's list. Finastra is a global core-banking company in a different segment and is not treated
> here.

## 3. The plan, in three phases

### Phase 1 — make what exists carry its own weight (now, and cheap)

Nothing here is a new page. All of it is making seventeen existing pages work harder.

- [x] **Homepage links microfinance**, and business lending — the latter had a page for some time
      while a stale code comment said it did not, so the strongest internal link a home page has was
      being withheld from it.
- [x] **The omnibus post links to its four children.** `due-diligence-what-lenders-ask` ranks 2.7
      and 10.8 off mentions; it now passes that relevance to the four pages that treat each
      properly. Highest-leverage single link on the site.
- [x] **Every new specialist post reaches a product or platform page** with a descriptive anchor.
- [x] **Tool cards on the new cluster.** Seven of today's eleven posts now carry a `tool:` card.
      Four deliberately do not — no calculator here answers a question the three state licensing
      pages or the collection-efficiency post raise, and a card pointing at a near-miss reads as a
      house ad.
- [ ] **Audit the other 41 posts for the same gap.** Only 8 of 52 posts link to `/tools/` in body
      text; 27 now carry the card. The rest should be checked one by one, not bulk-edited.

### Phase 2 — the two things that compound (weeks)

**Repeat the proven shape.** More operational long-tail posts, chosen the way the four were: a term
already surfacing from a *mention*, with thin competition, written as the practitioner's question.
Candidates from the same Search Console evidence: `unrealised intt reversed meaning` (position 11.0
— a post exists at 7.8, so this is a deepening, not a new page), provisioning by asset class,
restructuring treatment, SMA-to-NPA transition arithmetic.

**Keep the regulatory pages current, and date them.** The entire advantage in §2.2 decays if these
pages age the way the ones they beat did. Every page already carries its sources with dates; the
discipline is to revisit when a direction changes and update the `updated:` field, which the content
loader already supports and which almost nothing here uses.

### Phase 3 — authority, which is the real constraint (months, and mostly not writing)

§0.3 is the finding this phase answers. The levers, honestly ranked:

1. **The tools are the linkable asset.** A free, correct, India-specific calculator is the one thing
   on this site another site would cite without being asked. Nobody in the competitor set has ten.
   Making them more findable — and more citable — is the highest-return authority work available.
2. **Directory listings.** The head term is held by GoodFirms, Capterra, SoftwareSuggest and
   listicles. The realistic move is to be *in* them rather than to outrank them. **This is a
   commercial decision and is Manoj's**, not mine — recorded, not acted on.
3. **The CS practice as a publishing identity.** The compliance cluster is written by a practising
   CS. That is citable in a way vendor content is not.

## 4. What this plan deliberately does not do

- **No location pages.** Intelligrow has 212 and it is a liability, not a moat.
- **No comparison pages naming competitors.** Ruled out by the brief, and separately: our own
  evaluation content already occupies that intent.
- **No pricing page.** `nbfc software price in india` is real demand and allcloud serves it. Pricing
  is reserved to Manoj — recorded, not acted on.
- **No eighth commercial-intent page.** See §0.3. The slots are full; the problem is elsewhere.
- **No forecast.** Eleven non-brand clicks is too small a base, and any multiplier on it would be
  invention.

## 5. How to tell whether this is working

At six weeks, in Search Console, two numbers and nothing else:

1. **Average position of the twelve pages published on 9 October.** Specialist posts inside the top
   ten and compliance pages somewhere near thirty is the result this analysis predicts — and if that
   is what happens, Phase 3 is the next work, not Phase 2.
2. **Non-brand clicks against the baseline of about eleven.**

**The trap to avoid is the one already paid for once:** the glossary drew 712 impressions and four
clicks at positions 72–88. Impressions are not the measure. Position is.
