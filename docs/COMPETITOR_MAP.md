# Competitor map — Indian lending software, Oct 2026

**Researched:** 9 October 2026. **Method:** each vendor's own site, read directly. Where a claim is
theirs, it is quoted. Nothing here is inferred from a directory listing or a review site.

**Why this exists:** a page that duplicates an incumbent's page competes on domain authority Lenviq
does not have. This file is the record of who holds what, so the pages we write go where nobody is.

## 2. The vocabulary they actually use

This is the part that matters mechanically, because the site currently contains almost none of it.

- **"NBFC software"** — universal. allcloud, cloudbankin, finsta, intelligrow, amytechnologies all
  have a page at or near that exact slug. **Lenviq has no `/nbfc-software` page.** This is the single
  most-contested term in the segment and also the one Lenviq is already closest to ranking for.
- **"Credit co-operative society software"** — finsta's H1, intelligrow's solution page,
  microfinancesoftware's audience. A real and named segment.
- **"Nidhi company software"** — cloudbankin (trial form), intelligrow (solutions), finsta, idspay,
  amytechnologies ("soon"). Four vendors address it; **ruled out for Lenviq on product grounds** and
  the ruling holds — see §5.
- **"JLG" / "SHG" / "group loan"** — finsta has `/jlg-loan-software` and `/shg-software` as separate
  pages; amytechnologies has both as products. allcloud says "Group Loans" and **never says JLG**.
- **"Pawn broking" / "girvi" / "jewel loan" / "pawn ticket"** — pawnsoftware.in owns this vocabulary
  outright, including `/girvi-software`.
- **"LOS / LMS / LCS / CBS"** — finsta spells all four out as products. The acronyms are the segment's
  native shorthand and are used without expansion.

## 3. Their proof, and whether it is real

Worth reading closely, because BRAND-1 §5.4 forbids Lenviq the cheapest kinds.

**Verifiable, or at least falsifiable:**
- allcloud: SOC 2 Type 2, ISO 27001, VAPT, "Great Place to Work Certified Nov 2025-Nov 2026", and
  Gartner/Capterra/G2/SoftwareSuggest rating badges. Also "110+ Lending businesses", "45 Lakh Loans
  empowered", "99.5% assured guaranteed uptime".
- cloudbankin: ISO 27001, 27701, 22301; named G2 "Fall 2026 High Performer" badges. **No customer
  count and no logos** — unusually restrained for this segment.
- idspay: ISO 27001, SOC 2 Type II, ISO 9001, "DPDP READY", "99.9% Uptime SLA".

**Asserted and unfalsifiable:**
- jaguarsoftwareindia: "150+ satisfied lender clients", "approximately 90% Companies in the north",
  and named client growth figures ("Au Financiers grew to a size of 3500 crore on our software").
  It calls the same client both "Au Financiers" and "AU Small Finance Bank Limited".
- pawnsoftware.in: "Trusted by 500+ Pawn Brokers", "2.5 Cr+ Active Gold Loan Accounts", and two
  named testimonials with percentage outcomes ("Overdue loans have dropped by 40%").
- microfinancesoftware.co.in: "Since 2007", "17+ Years Experience", six unnamed client logos, and
  counters for Branches/Employees/Customers/Portfolio **with no figures in them**.
- finsta: 16 named client logos and three named testimonials — the most specific naming in the set.
- intelligrow: **no customers, no logos, no certifications named at all.** Its homepage dashboard
  shows "Active loans 12,840" and "Branches connected 46", which read as product mockup rather than
  client data.

**What this means for Lenviq:** the segment's median proof is an unverifiable number. A site that
instead shows legal name, CIN, GSTIN, registered office and a working phone number is not
competing on the same axis — and §8 of the brief is right that `nbfc software in kolkata` is a
request for a checkable vendor rather than a local one.

## 4. Where nobody is strong — the gaps worth taking

### 4.1 Microfinance regulation. The widest gap found.

**allcloud's own microfinance page has no regulatory content whatsoever.** Checked directly: no
mention of the RBI Microfinance Directions 2022, no 50%-of-household-income cap, no household income
assessment, no collateral-free requirement, no pricing or interest-rate disclosure rules. It says
"Group Loans", "group leaders", "CGT", "GRT" and a "Centre to Zone" hierarchy — operationally
literate, regulatorily silent.

The others are thinner still: microfinancesoftware.co.in is a feature list; amytechnologies sells
SHG and JLG as separate products with no regulation named.

**So the microfinance page in §5 of the brief is not a crowded entry — it is an empty one**, provided
it does what the brief says and proves itself on the 2022 Directions. Enter on JLG and group loans,
which is their vocabulary; prove on the cap and the household income assessment, which none of them
mention.

### 4.2 Compliance — but not against the competitors the brief expected

This is a **correction to the brief**, and it changes the plan rather than the priority.

The brief says of the compliance cluster: "the one nobody else on that competitor list can match."
That is true and also not the relevant test. **The vendors are not who holds those SERPs.** For
`nbfc compliance checklist` and `nbfc compliance calendar`, the results are compliance-service and
legal-content sites: `incorpx.io`, `lawrbit.com`, `startupgrantsindia.com`, `kanakkupillai.com`,
`vinodkothari.com`, plus data-filing vendors `datatracks.com` and `nelito.com`, plus a Scribd upload.
Lawrbit's NBFC compliance calendar was updated **May 2026** and is a genuinely good page.

And one competitor IS already here: **cloudbankin has a post, "Navigating Through The NBFC Compliance
Filings & Returns."** So the field is not empty.

**Where they are stronger:** domain authority, and in Vinod Kothari's case a genuinely superior
regulatory practice. Writing a better-phrased checklist than Lawrbit is not a plan.

**Where they are stale, which is the opening.** The authoritative text is the **Master Direction —
Reserve Bank of India (Filing of Supervisory Returns) Directions, 2024** (RBI/DoS.DSG/2023-24/110 and
DoS.DSG.No.10/33.01.001/2023-24, **27 February 2024**), which **repealed** the Master Direction —
NBFC Returns (Reserve Bank) Directions, 2016 and harmonised every filing timeline. Almost nothing in
those SERPs is built on it:

- Vinod Kothari's indexed year-end checklist is **FY 2021-22**.
- The RNA calendar cites 2008–2010 circulars.
- The RBI annex that still surfaces in search for "which NBFC files which return" is **dated 2007**.
- Datatracks and Nelito **disagree with each other** on whether DNBS02 is quarterly or annual. Under
  the 2024 Directions it moved from annual-within-60-days to **quarterly within 21 days**, so both
  are reporting a pre-2024 position and one of them is simply wrong.

**So the route around them is currency, not craft.** A page whose every timeline comes from the 2024
Directions, with the frequency table quoted from RBI's own text, is more accurate than the pages
above — and that is a claim about the source, not about the writing, which makes it defensible.

### 4.3 State money lending — real, and nobody has the registers

pawnsoftware.in is the only site in the set doing content-led acquisition, and it does it well:
`/guides/pawnbroking-license-india`, four free calculators, comparison pages, a "Knowledge Hub".
It is the closest thing in this segment to what Lenviq already does with `/tools`.

But its licensing coverage stops at the **Pawn Brokers Acts** — "pawn brokers must obtain a license
under the respective State Pawn Brokers Act", "In Tamil Nadu, pawn brokers are licensed under the
Tamil Nadu Pawn Brokers Act". It does **not** mention the state **Money-Lenders** Acts, and the word
"registers" does not appear on it at all.

That is precisely the §6.2 gap: the prescribed registers, receipts, interest ceilings and returns to
a licensing authority. Nobody in this set covers them.

### 4.4 Tools

Lenviq has ten working calculators. In this whole set: intelligrow lists "Calculators" under
Resources; pawnsoftware has four. Nobody else has any. **This is an existing, unrecognised
advantage** and the specialist posts in §7 should link into it rather than standing alone.

## 6. What this changes about tonight's plan

1. **§6 keeps its priority but changes its spine.** Every timeline from the 2024 Directions, quoted.
   The differentiator is currency against stale incumbents, not that vendors cannot write.
2. **§5's microfinance page is a stronger bet than the brief assumed.** The incumbents' pages are

3. **A `/nbfc-software` page is the one obvious missing door** — five competitors have it, Lenviq

   outside the brief's section list and is a positioning decision rather than a content one.
4. **§7's posts should link into `/tools`.** Ten calculators against a field of four is an advantage
   nobody is contesting.

## 7. State money lending (§6.2) — what was written and what was skipped

**Written: Tamil Nadu only, and read from the Act itself.** `content/blog/tamil-nadu-money-lending-licence.md`,
draft. The primary text was obtained (the Act's published PDF, decompressed and read directly) and
every quotation on the page is from it: s.3(1) and 3(2) on the licence per place of business, s.3(3)
on who holds it by entity form, s.5 on change of premises, s.7(1) as substituted by TN Act 41 of
1979, s.7(2) and 7(3) on charges, s.9(1)(a)–(d) and 9(2) on records and returns, s.14 to s.17 on
suspension, publication and penalty.

**The finding that justifies the page:** the Tamil Nadu interest ceiling **is not in the Act**.
s.7(1) says the Government fixes it by notification, correlated to RBI's bank rates. Every secondary
guide that quotes a percentage "under the Act" is quoting a notification without its date, or the
pre-1979 position. That is a correction nobody in the competitor set is making — pawnsoftware.in,
the only content-led site in the segment, covers the **Pawn Brokers** Acts and never mentions the
**Money-Lenders** Acts, and the word "registers" does not appear on it at all.

**Skipped: Kerala (1958) and West Bengal (1940), and the reason is not time.** I could obtain and
verify the Tamil Nadu primary text; I could not do the same for those two within this session. A
state licensing page is published under a practising Company Secretary's name, and a page built
from secondary summaries of a state Act is precisely the thing the brief says one wrong regulatory
claim costs more than five correct pages earn. **One state verified beats three states inferred.**

They remain worth writing, in this order, and the test is the same: obtain the Act, read it, quote
it. Kerala first — the southern concentration that makes Tamil Nadu worth doing applies to it too.

**What the Tamil Nadu page does NOT do, deliberately:** it carries no software pitch. The brief is
right that this reader wants an adviser, and a licensing guide with a product bolted onto it reads
as what it is. The only internal links are to the NBFC registration page and the compliance
checklist, both of which are the adjacent question rather than a sale.
